NIT Functions

Background

The Ocean Observatories Initiative deploys the Sea-Bird Scientific SUNA V2 (Submersible Ultraviolet Nitrate Analyzer) as its nitrate sensor. The SUNA V2 is an optical, chemical-free sensor derived from the ISUS (In Situ Ultraviolet Spectroscopy) technology developed at MBARI. Prior to 2017, OOI deployed the ISUS sensor on fixed depth platforms in the uncabled arrays. These were all converted to the SUNA V2. The OOI instrument class NUTNR (Nutrient Sensor) covers both the original ISUS instruments and the SUNA V2.

NUTNR-N Sea-Bird Scientific (formerly Satlantic) Deep SUNA Pioneer coastal AUVs Nutrient, Nitrate

nit_functions.py computes a single L2 data product, NITRTSC_L2, from raw UV absorption spectra produced by NUTNR instruments.

Within the OOI data system, NIT data products fall under the Water Column sampling regime (see the complete list of OOI data products).

All calibration coefficients are from the factory calibration file supplied with each instrument.

This page documents the algorithms used to compute the NIT data products. For general guidance on working with OOI biogeochemical sensor data, see the OOI Biogeochemical Sensor Data Best Practices and User Guide; for locating calibration coefficients and raw inputs, see Calibration Coefficients and Using These Functions with OOI Data.

Primary Sources

No OOI Data Product Specification exists for the NUTNR data products. The algorithm documented here is derived from the source code, its embedded comments, and the two journal articles cited below.

NITRTSC_L2 -- Temperature and Salinity Corrected Dissolved Nitrate

NITRTSC_L2 is the dissolved nitrate concentration (\(\mu\)M) corrected for the temperature and salinity dependence of the seawater UV absorption spectrum. It is produced by the NUTNR instrument class using the Sakamoto et al. (2009) algorithm, with the temperature correction of the seawater extinction coefficients updated to Plant et al. (2023).

L0 Data and Frame Types

The SUNA V2 produces two frame types in each measurement cycle:

  • Light frames (frame type SLB) -- UV absorption spectra measured through the seawater sample. These are used to compute NITRTSC_L2.
  • Dark frames (frame type SDB, SDF, or NDF) -- baseline measurements with the UV lamp off. These are not used by OOI; they are filled with NaN on output.

Each frame contains a 256-element UV absorption spectrum (NITROPT_L0) covering approximately 190-400 nm, sampled at roughly 0.8 nm intervals. The instrument also provides a dark current scalar (dark_value) averaged from the previous dark frame measurements.

NITRTSC_L2 Algorithm

The algorithm follows Sakamoto et al. (2009), with the temperature correction in Step 4 from Plant et al. (2023). For each light frame \(i\), the computation proceeds as follows.

Step 1 -- Select the spectral fitting window. Wavelength bins and their corresponding calibration arrays are subset to channels where \(\text{wllower} \leq \lambda \leq \text{wlupper}\):

\[\lambda,\ ENO3,\ ESWA,\ DI \gets \text{channels where } \text{wllower} \leq wl \leq \text{wlupper}\]

where \(wl\) is the wavelength (nm) of each of the 256 spectral channels; \(ENO3\) is the nitrate molar absorptivity (extinction coefficient) at each wavelength, determined from laboratory nitrate standards; \(ESWA\) is the seawater extinction coefficient at each wavelength, at a reference salinity of 35 and the calibration temperature \(T_{cal}\); and \(DI\) is the deionized water reference spectrum measured in the laboratory at the time of calibration. All four are 256-element arrays from the instrument's factory calibration file. wllower and wlupper are the limits (nm) of the fitting window, set by OOI according to the probe tip pathlength (217-240 nm for the 1-cm tip, 220-245 nm for the 4-cm tip) and supplied with the instrument's calibration values.

Step 2 -- Dark-correct the measured spectrum by subtracting the dark current scalar from the raw spectral counts:

\[SW_{corr} = SW - dark\_value\]

where \(SW\) is the raw spectrum (NITROPT_L0) and \(dark\_value\) is the dark current scalar, both reported by the instrument.

Step 3 -- Compute absorbance from the ratio of the deionized water reference to the dark-corrected seawater spectrum:

\[A = \log_{10}\!\left(\frac{DI}{SW_{corr}}\right)\]

Step 4 -- Temperature-correct the seawater extinction coefficients. The seawater extinction coefficients, determined at the calibration temperature \(T_{cal}\), are corrected to the in situ temperature \(T\) from the co-located CTD following Plant et al. (2023), Equations 6 and 8. The temperature response is a fourth-order polynomial in the scaled wavelength \(\lambda' = \lambda - 210\):

\[\begin{align} f(\lambda') &= c_0 + c_1 \times \lambda' + c_2 \times {\lambda'}^2 \\ &\quad + c_3 \times {\lambda'}^3 + c_4 \times {\lambda'}^4 \end{align}\]

with the fixed coefficients from Plant et al. (2023):

\[\begin{align} c_0 &= 1.46380 \times 10^{-2} \\ c_1 &= 1.67660 \times 10^{-3} \\ c_2 &= 2.91898 \times 10^{-5} \\ c_3 &= -7.56395 \times 10^{-6} \\ c_4 &= 1.27353 \times 10^{-7} \end{align}\]
\[ESWA_T = ESWA \times \exp\!\left(f(\lambda') \times (T - T_{cal})\right)\]

where \(T_{cal}\) (\(^\circ\)C) is the calibration temperature (cal_temp) from the factory calibration file and \(T\) (\(^\circ\)C) is the in situ temperature from the co-located CTD.

Plant et al. (2023) developed this model to replace the temperature correction of Sakamoto et al. (2009), Equation 4, which can bias nitrate high by 1-2 \(\mu\)mol kg\(^{-1}\) in waters warmer than the calibration temperature; the updated model reduces this bias by at least 50%. The pressure correction in Plant et al. (2023), Equation 9, is not applied.

Step 5 -- Subtract the seawater bromide contribution from the measured absorbance. The seawater absorbance is proportional to practical salinity \(S_P\):

\[A_{swa} = S_P \times ESWA_T\]
\[A_{comp} = A - A_{swa}\]

where \(S_P\) is the practical salinity from the co-located CTD.

Step 6 -- Solve for nitrate concentration by linear least squares. The model matrix \(M\) includes the nitrate extinction spectrum, a constant baseline offset, and a linear wavelength baseline:

\[M = \begin{bmatrix} ENO3 & \mathbf{1}/100 & \lambda/1000 \end{bmatrix}\]

The solution vector \(C\) is obtained via the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse:

\[C = M^{+} \times A_{comp}\]

The first element \(C[0]\) is NITRTSC_L2, the nitrate concentration in \(\mu\)M. The second and third elements capture a linear baseline that accounts for residual spectral interference.

Full algorithm derivations, calibration procedures, and source references are listed in the References section.

Source code in ion_functions/data/nit_functions.py 
def ts_corrected_nitrate(cal_temp, wl, eno3, eswa, di, dark_value, ctd_t,
                         ctd_sp, data_in, frame_type, wllower=217,
                         wlupper=240):
    """
    Compute NITRTSC_L2, dissolved nitrate concentration corrected for
    temperature and salinity.

    Parameters
    ----------
    cal_temp : array_like, shape (N,)
        Calibration water temperature [degC]. Scalar value tiled to length
        N by the OOI data management system.
    wl : array_like, shape (N, 256)
        Wavelength bins for each spectral channel [nm].
    eno3 : array_like, shape (N, 256)
        Nitrate molar absorptivity (extinction coefficients) at each
        wavelength, from factory calibration [1/(M cm)].
    eswa : array_like, shape (N, 256)
        Seawater extinction coefficients at each wavelength at reference
        salinity 35 and calibration temperature cal_temp, from factory
        calibration [1/(M cm)].
    di : array_like, shape (N, 256)
        Deionized water reference spectrum from factory calibration
        [counts].
    dark_value : array_like, shape (N,)
        Dark current scalar, averaged from dark frame measurements
        [counts].
    ctd_t : array_like, shape (N,)
        In situ water temperature from co-located CTD (TEMPWAT_L1)
        [degC].
    ctd_sp : array_like, shape (N,)
        Practical salinity from co-located CTD (PRACSAL_L2) [unitless].
    data_in : array_like, shape (N, 256)
        Raw UV absorption spectrum from the SUNA V2 (NITROPT_L0)
        [counts].
    frame_type : array_like, shape (N,)
        Frame type string for each data packet. Light frames ('SLB') are
        processed; dark frames ('SDB', 'SDF', 'NDF') are filled with NaN.
    wllower : float or array_like, shape (N,), optional
        Lower wavelength limit for spectral fitting window [nm]. Default
        is 217 nm (1-cm pathlength probe tip). Use 220 nm for 4-cm
        pathlength probe tips.
    wlupper : float or array_like, shape (N,), optional
        Upper wavelength limit for spectral fitting window [nm]. Default
        is 240 nm (1-cm pathlength probe tip). Use 245 nm for 4-cm
        pathlength probe tips.

    Returns
    -------
    NO3_conc : ndarray, shape (N,)
        Temperature and salinity corrected dissolved nitrate concentration
        (NITRTSC_L2) [uM]. Dark frame records are filled with NaN.

    Notes
    -----
    Absorbance is the log ratio of the deionized water reference to the
    dark-corrected spectrum. Sea salt absorbance, from the seawater
    extinction coefficients corrected to in situ temperature (Plant et al.
    2023) and scaled by practical salinity, is subtracted, and nitrate is
    fit by least squares together with a linear baseline in wavelength.
    """
    n_data_packets = data_in.shape[0]

    # make sure that the dimensionalities of wllower and wlupper are consistent
    # regardless of whether or not they are specified in the argument list.
    if np.isscalar(wllower):
        wllower = np.tile(wllower, n_data_packets)

    if np.isscalar(wlupper):
        wlupper = np.tile(wlupper, n_data_packets)

    # coefficients of the 4th-order polynomial in (wavelength - 210 nm) from
    # Plant et al. 2023 that describes the temperature dependence of the
    # absorbance of sea salt
    Aplant = 1.46380e-02
    Bplant = 1.67660e-03
    Cplant = 2.91898e-05
    Dplant = -7.56395e-06
    Eplant = 1.27353e-07

    NO3_conc = np.ones(n_data_packets)

    for i in range(0, n_data_packets):

        if frame_type[i] == 'SDB' or frame_type[i] == 'SDF' or frame_type[i] == "NDF":

            # change this to output nans instead.
            NO3_conc[i] = np.nan

        else:

            # Find wavelength bins that fall between the upper and lower
            # limits for spectra fit
            useindex = np.logical_and(wllower[i] <= wl[i, :], wl[i, :] <= wlupper[i])

            # subset data so that we only use wavelengths between wllower & wlupper
            WL = wl[i, useindex]
            ENO3 = eno3[i, useindex]
            ESWA = eswa[i, useindex]
            DI = np.array(di[i, useindex], dtype='float64')
            SW = np.array(data_in[i, useindex], dtype='float64')

            # correct each SW intensity for dark current
            SWcorr = SW - dark_value[i]

            # calculate absorbance
            Absorbance = np.log10(DI / SWcorr)

            # now estimate molar absorptivity of seasalt at in situ temperature
            # use Satlantic calibration and correct as in Plant et al. 2023.
            WLs = WL - 210.0
            TcorPoly = Aplant + Bplant * WLs + Cplant * WLs**2 + Dplant * WLs**3 + Eplant * WLs**4
            SWA_Ext_at_T = ESWA * np.exp(TcorPoly * (ctd_t[i] - cal_temp[i]))

            # absorbance due to sea salt
            A_SWA = ctd_sp[i] * SWA_Ext_at_T
            # subtract seasalt absorbance from measured absorbance
            Acomp = np.array(Absorbance - A_SWA, ndmin=2).T

            # ENO3 plus a linear baseline
            subset_array_size = np.shape(ENO3)
            # for the constant in the linear baseline
            Ones = np.ones((subset_array_size[0],), dtype='float64') / 100
            M = np.vstack((ENO3, Ones, WL / 1000)).T

            # C has NO3, baseline constant, and slope (vs. WL)
            C = np.dot(np.linalg.pinv(M), Acomp)

            NO3_conc[i] = C[0, 0]

    return NO3_conc

Additional Notes

Dark frames (frame types SDB, SDF, and NDF) produce NaN on output. Light frames (frame type SLB) are processed through the algorithm described in the Background.

The wllower and wlupper arguments default to 217 nm and 240 nm respectively, matching the 1-cm pathlength probe tip specification. For 4-cm pathlength probe tips, the OOI system supplies 220 nm and 245 nm via the time-vectorized calibration coefficient mechanism.

The calibration arrays are time-vectorized by the OOI data system: each 256-element array is tiled to shape \((N, 256)\), where \(N\) is the number of data packets, so that the coefficients can vary across deployments (and instruments) within a single input array. The fitting window and coefficients are therefore evaluated for each data packet.
